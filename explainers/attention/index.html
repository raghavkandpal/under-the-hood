Attention

The mechanism at the heart of every large language model. Each word asks a question, every word offers an answer, and each word takes in a blend of the others, weighted by how well they match.

1. A soft dictionary lookup

A normal dictionary returns the value of the one key that matches exactly. Attention compares the query with every key (a dot product), turns the scores into weights that sum to 1 (softmax), and returns a weighted blend of all the values. Here the values are colours. Drag the query arrow, or the keys (or tab to one and use the arrow keys).

Low temperature gives almost a hard lookup: the best match takes everything. High temperature spreads attention evenly. Real transformers divide scores by √d so that longer vectors don't make softmax too sharp.

2. Self-attention over a sentence

In a transformer, every word produces its own query, key and value. A head is a pair of learned projections that decides what "matching" means. Why it matters

Context. On its own, the vector for "it" means nothing in particular. After attention it's a blend that includes "cat" or "ball", so the next layer knows what "it" refers to. Stack dozens of layers and the vectors carry richer and richer context.

Ambiguity. In the first example, the coreference head splits "it" between cat and ball. One shallow head can't tell which is meant. That takes world knowledge (balls aren't happy, boxes are heavy), which deeper layers build up. Try the "heavy" sentence. This head makes the same animate-first guess and gets it wrong, because it only knows word types, not meanings.

Parallelism. Every word attends to every other word at once, as one big matrix multiplication. That's what made transformers so much faster to train than older models that read one word at a time. It's also why cost grows with the square of the text length.

The causal mask is how GPT-style models generate text. Each position may only look at earlier ones, so the model can be trained to predict the next word without seeing it.
