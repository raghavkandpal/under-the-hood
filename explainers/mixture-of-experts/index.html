Mixture of experts

A mixture-of-experts layer holds many feed-forward networks but sends each token through only a few of them, so a model can have far more parameters than it spends compute on. The catch is the router: left alone, it piles tokens onto a favourite expert. Here you can watch routing, count the parameters, and train a router to share the work.

How it works

Routing. A small linear layer, the router, scores every expert for each token. A softmax turns the scores into probabilities, the layer keeps the top k, and the kept probabilities are divided by their sum so the weights add to 1. The token's output is the weighted sum of those k experts' outputs. Every other expert does no work for that token. Like the KV cache, it trades memory for compute. Compare with attention, where each token looks at all the earlier tokens.

Total vs active parameters. The experts replace the single feed-forward block in a transformer layer, while attention and embeddings stay shared. Total parameters are shared + E × expert, but a token only touches shared + k × expert. The bars count 2 units for the shared part and 1 per expert. Add experts at a fixed k and the total grows while the compute per token stays the same. That is the trade: more capacity for the same cost per token, paid for in memory, since all the experts must be loaded.

Capacity and dropped tokens. Experts run on batches, so each has a fixed number of slots: capacity factor × tokens ÷ experts. A token routed to a full expert is dropped, and passes through the layer unchanged on the residual path. An untrained router with a bias towards one expert overflows it at once, and raising the capacity factor drops fewer tokens but reserves slots that go unused.

The auxiliary loss. The Switch Transformer adds E · Σ fᵢ·Pᵢ to the training loss, where fᵢ is the fraction of tokens sent to expert i and Pᵢ is the average router probability for it. It is about 1 when load is even and rises towards E when one expert takes everything. Gradients flow through Pᵢ, the soft part, which nudges the router towards sharing. Step through training above: with the loss off, the router follows what the tokens prefer and keeps overloading its favourites, and with a large weight the busiest expert's share falls, and at weight 10 and capacity 1.25 nothing is dropped in this run. Weights 1 and 3 help less.
