Paxos

Five servers must agree on one value even though servers crash and messages arrive late. Paxos does it with two rounds of questions, and a rule that lets a later proposer discover and keep anything that might already have been decided.

    How it works

    Two phases. A proposer picks a ballot number and asks the acceptors to prepare: each one promises to ignore anything below that ballot, and reports the highest-ballot value it has already accepted, if any. With promises from a majority (3 of 5), the proposer sends accept with a value. Each acceptor takes it unless it has since promised a higher ballot. When a majority has accepted the same ballot, the value is chosen.

    The one rule that matters. If any promise reports an accepted value, the proposer must propose the one with the highest ballot instead of its own. Any two majorities share an acceptor, so a later proposer always hears about a chosen value. Open Second proposer: v is chosen at ballot 1, and ballot 2 asks for w through each of the 10 possible three-server groups. Every one finds v and chooses v again.

    Try it. Switch to "v on 1 acceptor". Now v was accepted but never chosen, and the answer depends on who B happens to hear from: 6 groups include that acceptor and keep v, 4 don't and choose w. Both are safe, since nothing had been decided.

    Failures. In Free play, click acceptors to crash them. With two down, a value is still chosen; with three down, nothing can be, and nothing wrong is chosen either. Paxos gives up progress, never agreement.

    Duelling proposers. Each proposer's accept is rejected because the other's prepare got there first, so each retries with a higher ballot and the other is rejected again. With the lock-step schedule in Duel, 20 rounds pass with no value chosen and the ballot reaches 40. Waiting a random number of rounds before a retry breaks the tie: across 500 seeds, a value was chosen by round 4 every time.

    Cost. One decision takes four message delays: prepare, promise, accept, accepted. Multi-Paxos runs the first phase once for a stable leader and covers every later slot with it, so each further decision takes two. That is the idea behind Raft's leader, and the reason it needs quorums to overlap.

    Simplified: one decision (not a log), messages delivered instantly in a scripted order, crashes that never recover, and no learner role or failure detector. Multi-Paxos is counted by formula.
