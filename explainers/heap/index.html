Heaps & priority queues

An emergency room doesn't treat patients in arrival order, a game engine fires whichever timer is due soonest, and a route planner always expands the closest unexplored spot next. Each needs “give me the smallest, fast” while new items keep arriving. A heap does both in a handful of steps, and it's just an array.

As stored: slot i's children at 2i+1 and 2i+2

How it works

The rule. Every parent is smaller than its children. That's all. Siblings can be in any order, so it's far looser than a sorted list, but it guarantees the smallest item sits at the top.

A tree with no pointers. The tree is always filled level by level, left to right, so it fits in an array with no gaps. Parent and children are found by arithmetic, which is compact and cache-friendly.

Push appends at the end, then sifts up: while it's smaller than its parent, swap. Pop takes the root, moves the last item into its place, then sifts down, swapping with the smaller child until the rule holds. Either way the item travels at most the tree's height, which is log₂ n: about 20 swaps for a million items.

Heapify builds a heap from scratch in linear time by sifting down from the last parent backwards. Most nodes are near the bottom and barely move. Pop everything and you get heapsort.

Used in Dijkstra's and A* pathfinding, OS schedulers, Huffman coding, timer wheels, merging sorted files, and “top 10 of a billion” queries: keep a heap of the best 10 and discard anything smaller than its root.
