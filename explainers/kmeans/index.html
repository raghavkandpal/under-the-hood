K-means clustering

Hand an algorithm a cloud of points with no labels and ask it to find groups. K-means is the classic answer: two simple steps repeated until nothing changes. It's also a good lesson in what "finding structure" can and can't mean.

The two steps

1. Assign. Every point joins its nearest centre. The faint background shows each centre's territory, its Voronoi cell.

2. Update. Every centre moves to the average of its points. Repeat. Each step can only lower the total squared distance, so the process always settles, usually within a few rounds.

Settling isn't the same as being right. K-means finds a local optimum that depends on where it started. With random starts on "5 blobs", press 🎲 a few times and sometimes two centres share a blob while two blobs share a centre. k-means++ picks starting centres far apart, which avoids most of these traps, and it's the default in practice.

What it can't see. K-means assumes round, similar-sized groups. On "Ring around a blob" no k gives the obvious answer, because a ring isn't a ball. On "Big + small blobs" it slices the big one rather than respecting the small ones. On "No real clusters" it still returns k groups. It always finds something, so the structure may only be in the algorithm.

Other methods handle those shapes better: DBSCAN (density), Gaussian mixtures (soft, elliptical clusters) and spectral clustering. Choosing k honestly also takes care: the elbow, silhouette scores, or knowledge of the problem.
