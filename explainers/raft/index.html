Raft consensus

Five servers, unreliable networks, machines that crash. How do they agree on one shared history? Raft elects a leader, the leader orders every write, and nothing counts until a majority has it. Break things and watch it recover.

The rules that make it work

Terms. Time is divided into numbered terms, each with at most one leader. Any message carrying a higher term makes the receiver update its own and fall back to follower. A stale leader can't do damage for long.

Elections. Followers expect regular heartbeats. If one's randomised timer runs out (the ring), it becomes a candidate, votes for itself, and asks the others. Each server gives one vote per term, and only to a candidate whose log is at least as up-to-date as its own. A majority wins. The random timeouts make split votes rare.

Replication. Clients talk to the leader, which appends to its log and sends the new entries with its heartbeats. Each entry carries the index and term of the one before it. A follower whose log doesn't match rejects it, and the leader backs up one entry and retries until they line up, then overwrites the follower's conflicting tail.

Commitment. Once a majority stores an entry from the leader's current term, it's committed. Any future leader must win votes from a majority, and any two majorities overlap, so every future leader already has the entry.

Try it: make a couple of writes, then Partition leader + 1 and immediately write again. That write lands on the old leader, which still thinks it's in charge, and it stays dashed because the old leader can't reach a majority. The other three elect a new leader in a higher term and keep committing. Now Heal: the old leader sees the higher term, steps down, and its uncommitted entries get overwritten. Nothing committed was ever lost.
