Multi-armed bandits

Two page designs convert at slightly different rates and you don't know which is better. Splitting traffic evenly learns the answer but keeps showing the worse design; a bandit shifts traffic toward the better one while it is still learning.

How it works

Design A converts at 10%, design B at 12%. In this model, 10,000 visitors arrive one at a time and each is shown one design. Every visit either converts or not. Against always showing B, a 50/50 split loses 5,000 × 0.02 = 100 expected conversions. That loss is the regret.

Beliefs as a Beta curve. Start each design at Beta(1, 1), a flat guess. Every conversion adds 1 to the first number and every miss adds 1 to the second. After 7 conversions in 10 visits you have Beta(8, 4), whose mean is 8/12 ≈ 0.667.

Thompson sampling. For each visitor, draw one plausible rate from each design's Beta curve and show the design with the higher draw. A design that looks better is drawn from more often, and an uncertain one still gets a turn. Over the 200 seeded runs here (seeds 1 to 200), the mean regret is about 23 conversions, against 100 for the split.

Greedy gets stuck. Greedy shows each design once, then always the one with the higher observed rate. An early lucky streak on A is never corrected, because B stops getting visits. Greedy sends most traffic to the worse design in 174 of the 200 runs and Thompson sampling in 2. The 50/50 split never does. Greedy's mean regret is about 174, worse than the split.

The price. Thompson sampling shows the better design more over time: on seed 1, 72.9% of the first 1,000 visitors and 96.4% of the last 1,000. The worse design gets fewer visits, so its estimate is vaguer. On seed 1, A's 95% interval is 4.7 points wide under Thompson sampling and 1.6 points wide under the split.

Try it. Pick Greedy and slide through seeds. Seed 1 is atypical: greedy happens to lock onto B there. Most seeds lock onto A.

In this model the rates are fixed, ties in greedy go to design A, and the interval is a normal approximation; the 200 runs and seeds are the only randomness. This page makes no claim about any real testing platform.
