Precision, recall & ROC

A spam filter, a fraud detector or a cancer screen doesn't really say yes or no. It gives a score, and someone picks a threshold. Move the threshold and you trade one kind of mistake for the other. Here's how to see that trade-off, and why "99% accurate" can mean nothing.

The vocabulary

Recall (sensitivity, true-positive rate): of all real positives, what share did we catch? Precision: of everything we flagged, what share was real? False-positive rate: of all real negatives, what share did we wrongly flag? F1 is the harmonic mean of precision and recall, one number that's high only when both are.

The threshold is a business decision. Slide it left and you catch more fraud (recall up) but annoy more honest customers (precision down). Slide it right and it's the reverse. No threshold is "correct". It depends on what a missed case costs against a false alarm.

ROC and AUC summarise the model over every threshold. AUC, the area under the ROC curve, is the probability that a random positive gets a higher score than a random negative: 0.5 is a coin flip, 1.0 is perfect. It's a good way to compare models before choosing a threshold.

The rare-case trap. Drop prevalence to 1%. The ROC curve barely moves, but precision collapses, because even a small false-positive rate on 99% of the data swamps the true hits (exactly Bayes' theorem). Meanwhile "accuracy" looks fantastic, since flagging nothing at all would score 99%. For rare events, look at precision and recall, not accuracy.
