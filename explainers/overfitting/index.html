Overfitting

What's going on

Underfitting (high bias). A straight line can't follow a wave, however much data you give it. Training and test error are both high, because the model is too simple for the pattern.

Overfitting (high variance). A degree-15 polynomial can pass through almost every training point, noise included. Training error drops toward zero while test error shoots up. Press 🎲 a few times and the wild curve changes completely with each sample. That instability is what "variance" means.

The sweet spot sits at the bottom of the green U. The usual ways to get there are to:

  • Hold out data to measure test error honestly. You can't see the green curve without a held-out set, which is why every ML project starts by splitting its data.
  • Get more data. Push "training points" to 80 and even degree 15 behaves, because the noise averages out.
  • Regularise. Raise λ to penalise large coefficients. The model can stay complex but is pushed toward smooth answers. Weight decay and dropout in neural networks are the same idea.
Try it: set degree 12 with 14 points and watch the curve go wild at the edges. Without changing the degree, raise λ to about 10-2. Test error falls by orders of magnitude.

Fits use least squares on a Legendre polynomial basis (numerically stabler than raw powers). Errors are mean squared error. The test set is 300 fresh points from the same process.
