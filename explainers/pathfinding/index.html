Pathfinding

How does a map app, or a game character, find the best route? Four classic algorithms explore the same grid in very different ways. Draw walls and mud, drag the start and goal around, and race them.

Four strategies

Breadth-first search explores in rings: everything 1 step away, then 2, then 3. It finds the path with the fewest steps but doesn't know that mud is slow.

Dijkstra always expands the cheapest-so-far cell, so the frontier spreads like a ripple that slows down in mud. It's guaranteed to find the cheapest path, but it explores in every direction, including away from the goal.

Greedy best-first only asks "which cell looks closest to the goal?" It's often very fast and often wrong. In the Trap preset it charges straight into the cup and has to back out. In the Swamp it wades straight through the mud and pays more than double the best cost.

A* combines the two, ranking cells by cost so far + estimated cost to go. As long as the estimate never overestimates (here, straight-line grid distance), A* is still guaranteed optimal, and it explores far less than Dijkstra. That's why it's the default in games and robotics, and why route planners build on the same idea.

Moves are up, down, left and right; open cells cost 1 and mud costs 6. The estimate is Manhattan distance, which never overestimates here.
