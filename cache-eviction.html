Cache eviction

Every cache, whether CPU, database buffer, CDN or browser, eventually fills up. Something must go to make room. Which item you throw out decides whether the next request is a fast hit or a slow miss, and the best rule depends on how your data is accessed.

The policies

FIFO evicts whatever was loaded first. It's trivial to implement, but it will throw out your most popular item just because it's old.

LRU (least recently used) evicts what hasn't been touched for longest, betting that recent use predicts future use. It's the default almost everywhere, but a one-off scan over lots of data flushes out the genuinely hot items, and a loop just bigger than the cache gets zero hits.

LFU (least frequently used) evicts what's been used least often. Hot items survive scans, but it's slow to notice when popularity shifts: yesterday's favourites linger. Try "Popularity shifts halfway".

Random is surprisingly decent and immune to the pathological loop.

Optimal (Belady's algorithm) evicts whatever will be needed furthest in the future. It's impossible in practice, but it's the yardstick: the gap between it and a real policy is how much room there is to improve. Modern caches such as ARC, 2Q, W-TinyLFU (used by Caffeine) and Redis's LFU mode blend recency and frequency to get closer.
