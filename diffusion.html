Diffusion models

Image generators such as Stable Diffusion learn one skill: take a slightly noisy picture and guess what it looked like before the noise. Apply that skill over and over, starting from pure static, and a brand-new image condenses out of nothing. Here it's 2-D points instead of pixels, so you can watch every one.

Forward: training points with noise added. Easy, no learning needed.

Reverse: start from pure noise and denoise step by step. This is the part that gets learned.

How it works

The forward process gradually mixes data with Gaussian noise. At noise level t a point is √ᾱ(t) × original + √(1 − ᾱ(t)) × noise. By t = 1 nothing of the original is left: every dataset ends up as the same featureless blob. This direction is trivial, and it's how training examples are made: take a real image, pick a random t, add that much noise.

The network learns to undo one bit of noise. Shown a noisy point and t, it predicts the clean version (or, equivalently, the noise that was added). Training is plain regression on millions of such pairs.

Generation runs time backwards. Start from pure noise. Ask the denoiser for its best guess of the clean point, step a little toward it, re-add the right amount of noise for the new level, and repeat. Early steps decide the coarse layout, which arm of the spiral, and late steps fine-tune. Fewer steps is faster but rougher. Drag “denoising steps” down to 2.

Memorisation vs creativity. The denoiser here isn't a neural network. It's the mathematically perfect denoiser for these training points, and perfection is a problem: every sample lands exactly on a training point. It can only copy. A real network can't represent that perfect function, and its smoothing (the “blur” slider) is exactly what lets it generate new points along the shape, the 2-D version of a novel image. When a real model memorises some training images too well, it reproduces them, a live research and copyright issue.

Text-to-image models condition the denoiser on a text embedding, so each step nudges toward “a cat in a hat”. They usually work in a compressed latent space rather than raw pixels, which is the “latent” in latent diffusion.
